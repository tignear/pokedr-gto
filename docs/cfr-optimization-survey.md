# CFR Optimization Survey

This note records optimization directions that look relevant to `pokedr-gto`.
It separates practical next steps from ideas that are important but unlikely to
fix the current bottleneck alone.

## What Commercial Solvers Publicly Say

PioSOLVER reports solution quality as exploitability per hand. Its public docs
state that 1bb/100 is an ordinary practical target and that many practical flop
trees can now be solved to decent accuracy in a few seconds on strong CPUs.

Relevant source:

- https://piosolver.com/docs/technical_details/
- https://piosolver.com/docs/viewer/numbers_in_piosolver/

GTO Wizard publicly describes two regimes:

- pre-solved libraries generated by standard CFR-style solvers, commonly
  targeting 0.1%-0.3% pot Nash Distance;
- fast custom/AI solves that combine traditional solving with predictive neural
  networks and lookahead, while river spots are solved exactly.

Their benchmark article claims a PioSolver comparison where PioSolver needed
4862 seconds on 16 cores and 128GB RAM to reach 0.23% accuracy for their test,
while GTO Wizard reached comparable accuracy in seconds with their AI path. This
is not a pure exact-CFR claim; it is an approximation stack.

Relevant source:

- https://blog.gtowizard.com/gto-wizard-ai-benchmarks/
- https://blog.gtowizard.com/how-solvers-work/
- https://blog.gtowizard.com/why-doesnt-my-solution-match-gto-wizard/

Takeaway for this project: exact full-runout CFR should remain as the validation
baseline, but a competitive real-time agent probably needs a second approximate
path. Commercial public claims point toward smarter trees, predictive values,
and targeted re-solving, not just faster full-tree enumeration.

## CFR Variants

CFR+ is a proven baseline. Tammelin reports order-of-magnitude computation-time
improvement over prior CFR variants in large imperfect-information games.

Relevant source:

- https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.5042

Discounted CFR is the most relevant current exact variant for this codebase.
Brown and Sandholm introduce regret/strategy discounting variants and report
variants that outperform CFR+ in all tested games, while keeping compatibility
with modern pruning techniques in important cases.

Relevant source:

- https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04040

Alternating updates matter. Burch, Moravcik, and Schmid revisit CFR+ and
alternating updates; this is a warning not to treat simultaneous and alternating
updates as interchangeable when comparing variants.

Relevant source:

- https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.11542

Hyperparameter schedules are worth testing because they are training-free and
cheap. Zhang, McAleer, and Sandholm propose dynamically adjusting the
discounting hyperparameter over time to accelerate CFR variants.

Relevant source:

- https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09097

Predictive/optimistic CFR is theoretically interesting but not an obvious first
fix for the current tree. Farina et al. obtain a better worst-case iteration
rate than classic CFR, but their own abstract says experiments were faster than
original CFR while not as fast as newer practical variants. Recent PDCFR-style
work is promising, but the strongest public poker-specific signal still suggests
benchmarking against DCFR/DCFR+ carefully before assuming PDCFR+ wins.

Relevant sources:

- https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~gfarina/2019/stable-predictive-icml19/stable-predictive.icml19.pdf
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08174

Takeaway for this project: default should stay DCFR+/scheduled DCFR+ until our
own exploitability curves show otherwise. PDCFR+ belongs behind a measured
sweep, not as an assumed default.

## Tree and Action Heuristics

Bet-size abstraction is not optional in no-limit poker. Hawkin, Holte, and
Szafron frame no-limit bet sizing as a continuous/many-valued action parameter
problem and show automated action abstraction can beat hand-crafted abstractions
in no-limit Leduc.

Relevant source:

- https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/7880

Safe and nested subgame solving directly addresses observed/off-tree bet sizes.
Brown and Sandholm show how to respond to opponent actions outside the original
action abstraction and repeat subgame solving as the game progresses.

Relevant source:

- https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.02955

Libratus' public descriptions match the user's target architecture: blueprint
strategy, real-time finer-grained subgame solving, and a self-improver that
detects missing opponent bet-size branches and adds them back into the blueprint.

Relevant source:

- https://www.cmu.edu/news/stories/archives/2017/december/ai-inner-workings.html

Takeaway for this project: "legal actions + standard sizes + observed size" is
a reasonable first abstraction, but the stronger direction is a persistent
blueprint plus off-tree action insertion/re-solving. The action set should be
treated as part of the solver state and diagnostics, not as a one-off local
choice.

## Pruning and Warm Starts

Regret-based pruning is one of the clearest exact-CFR speedups. Brown and
Sandholm show paths with sufficiently negative regret can be skipped until the
earliest iteration they could become positive, preserving CFR convergence and
giving order-of-magnitude speedups in experiments.

Relevant source:

- https://papers.neurips.cc/paper/5910-regret-based-pruning-in-extensive-form-games

Warm starts are useful when the starting strategy is credible. The 2016
strategy-based warm-start paper focuses on initializing a large game from a
coarser abstraction/rough equilibrium. More recent warm-start papers exist, but
many target adversarial team games rather than two-player postflop poker.

Relevant source:

- https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~sandholm/www/warmStart.aaai16.withAppendixAndTypoFix.pdf

Takeaway for this project: pruning is more likely to produce a meaningful exact
speedup than another terminal-CFV micro-variant. Warm starts become attractive
after we have a reliable smaller-tree or prior-flop policy source.

## GPU/Parallel CFR

Recent parallel-CFR work continues to frame practical real-time play as a
few-seconds-per-decision problem where the number of CFR iterations completed in
that window directly affects strength. This supports measuring
bb/100-improvement-per-second rather than only per-iteration quality.

Relevant source:

- https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19928

Takeaway for this project: optimizing the exact terminal CFV kernel is still
valuable, but it is only one axis. Solver-level throughput should be reported as
exploitability reduction per wall-clock second, with GPU setup separated from
steady-state iteration cost.

## Recommended Order

1. Add a small benchmark harness that reports steady-state `bb/100` improvement
   per second for CFR+, DCFR+, scheduled DCFR+, and PDCFR+.
2. Implement regret-based pruning for low-reach/negative-regret public branches,
   guarded by exact exploitability checks.
3. Make action abstraction diagnostics first-class: action set, observed sizes,
   merge decisions, and off-tree inserted sizes should be visible in tree DB.
4. Keep exact full-runout terminal CFV as baseline; create approximate/predictive
   paths only when their error can be evaluated against the exact path.
5. Revisit GPU terminal CFV only with a board/group-major design that avoids
   materializing `52 * combo_count` card-prefix lanes.
